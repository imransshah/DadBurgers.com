## Overview
In part 1, Daniella Palca of OPM's Workforce Policy and Innovation Center covers the regulatory basis and OPM's June 17, 2025 reforms. Part 2, by an unnamed narrator, covers executive practice for plans, evaluations and poor performance. Timestamps are marked P1 or P2; each part restarts at 00:00.

## Key points
- Performance management is a cycle: plan, monitor, develop, rate, reward, and act on poor performance. Appraisal systems are OPM-approved policy frameworks, not software. [P1 01:11]
- Under the new guidance, supervisors hold formal check-ins at least quarterly, and all agencies move to an October 1–September 30 cycle by October 1, 2026. [P1 03:13]
- Implementation plans cover communications, timing, explaining element and rating changes, linking ratings to awards and consequences, and compliance monitoring. [P1 07:19]
- Non-SES supervisors receive a mandatory critical element, Holding Employees Accountable, used exactly as written. [P1 10:27]
- Plans should leave "no mystery about what it takes to be fully successful versus outstanding" (Palca); at least one critical element aligns to an organizational goal or administration priority. [P1 15:32]
- Requirements describe outcomes across quantity, quality, timeliness and cost; SES results elements should include at least one quality measure. [P2 07:17]
- Ratings need evidence and a specific narrative; under a normalized distribution most employees are Level 3. [P2 14:23]
- Recognition should reward outcomes, not tenure, popularity or rotation. [P2 17:29]
- Poor performance starts with diagnosis, documentation, a candid conversation and an improvement plan; formal action involves HR and counsel. [P2 20:35]

## Executive takeaways
- Specific, evidence-based rating levels make differentiated ratings defensible. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
- Quarterly check-ins and documented feedback are the base of both recognition and accountability. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Operational metrics (resolution time, quality reviews, customer satisfaction) fit the quantity, quality, timeliness and cost dimensions. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
