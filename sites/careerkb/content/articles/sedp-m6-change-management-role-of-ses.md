## Overview
David Bernhardt, former Secretary of the Interior who also served there as Deputy Secretary and Solicitor, talks with host Rebecca about the career executive's role in change. He draws on two decades at Interior, covering political-career partnership, decision accountability, implementation and culture.

## Key points
- Early on, Bernhardt kept a career executive whom others wanted reassigned after she said her client was the Secretary; their partnership shaped his later work. [05:12]
- His model: policymakers set the what, and career executives and the civil service set the how, within law, facts, process and funding. [08:16]
- Career staff add value with history and candid pros and cons; the policymaker decides and should take the criticism, as he describes doing on COVID-era park decisions. [11:22]
- Career executives should help new appointees understand statutory authority and delegations, and should study the new administration's announced policies, which the speaker says now arrive early through executive orders. [19:34]
- The speaker argues the President alone represents the will of the people in the executive branch, so the civil service should reflect that direction within the law. [21:36]
- Executives are generalists who lead by example; he read staff suggestions weekly and stresses outcomes over activities. [23:39]
- He says secretaries are remembered for defining crises and need to be armed with "lions, not lambs." [31:51]
- For contested legal opinions he used teams arguing dueling interpretations; he credits that process with the opinions surviving the next administration. [34:53]
- Lasting change needs a stated reason, a repeated goal, time to lock it in, institutional structure (his consolidation of Interior's ethics function) and deadlines with accountability. [37:55]

## Executive takeaways
- Execute lawful direction faithfully and know the authority and delegations behind each decision. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- Make change durable through structure, deadlines and repetition rather than personal effort alone. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Lead by example, keep upward channels open for frontline ideas, and address poor performance directly. (ECQ4 Leading People)
