## Overview
In part 1, Daniella Palca of OPM's Workforce Policy and Innovation Center covers the regulatory basis and OPM's June 17, 2025 reforms. Part 2, by an unnamed narrator, covers executive practice for plans, evaluations and poor performance. Timestamps are marked P1 or P2; each part restarts at 00:00.

## Key points
- Performance management is a cycle: plan, monitor, develop, rate, reward, and act on poor performance [P1 01:11]. Appraisal systems are OPM-approved policy frameworks, not software. [P1 02:12]
- Under the new guidance, supervisors hold formal check-ins at least quarterly [P1 03:13], and all agencies are on or moving to an October 1–September 30 cycle [P1 04:16], with changes in place by October 1, 2026. [P1 08:22]
- Implementation plans cover communications, timing, explaining element and rating changes, linking ratings to awards and consequences, and compliance monitoring. [P1 07:19]
- Non-SES supervisors receive a mandatory critical element, Holding Employees Accountable [P1 10:27], used exactly as written. [P1 16:34]
- Plans should leave "no mystery about what it takes to be fully successful versus outstanding" (Palca); at least one critical element aligns to an organizational goal or administration priority. [P1 16:34]
- Requirements describe outcomes and outputs, not just activities [P2 05:14]; results are measured by quantity, quality, timeliness and cost effectiveness, and SES results elements should include at least one quality measure. [P2 07:17]
- Ratings need evidence and a specific narrative; under a normalized distribution most employees are Level 3. [P2 14:23]
- Recognition should reward outcomes, not tenure, popularity or rotation. [P2 17:29]
- Poor performance starts with diagnosis, documentation, a candid conversation and an improvement plan [P2 20:35]; formal steps are taken in consultation with HR and legal advisors. [P2 21:36]

## Executive takeaways
- The course ties normalized ratings to plans that define each rating level and to written evidence for every rating. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
- The course presents regular check-ins and documented feedback as the way to recognize achievement and catch problems early. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Results are framed by quantity, quality, timeliness and cost effectiveness, with quality measures such as accuracy, stakeholder satisfaction or adherence to standards. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
