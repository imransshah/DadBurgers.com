## Overview
Micah Block, Deputy Assistant Secretary for Strategic Communications at the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, covers the limits on media engagement by career senior executives, interview practice, and internal communication. The session assumes most career executives rarely speak to the press.

## Key points
- External messages should align with the department's mission and priorities and stay inside its boundaries. [01:09]
- The speaker cites a California immigration enforcement operation that, in DHS's account, early coverage mischaracterized; he says a social media response shifted coverage within 24 hours. [03:11]
- A career executive contacted by a reporter goes first to leadership and the Office of Public Affairs. Factual remarks, statements framed as personal, and likes or reposts can all be reported as official. [05:14]
- Information flows up from operational staff; messaging flows down from leadership. [09:21]
- Interview preparation includes anticipating negative questions, murder boards with hostile mock questioners, a 15-second statement, one thesis, and practiced bridges. [13:26]
- DHS records its interviews; the speaker says posting a full recording countered a network's misleading edit and led to an apology. [16:30]
- The truth sandwich: lead with facts, address the false claim, end with facts, in a conversational tone. [17:32]
- A daily all-office call followed by team meetings keeps work aligned and reduces rework. [21:38]
- In a crisis, leaders pass down urgency and action steps, not stress, which otherwise cascades into rushed work and more edits. [24:40]

## Executive takeaways
- Media contact by career executives runs through leadership and public affairs, with approved messaging and written guidance. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- In a crisis, leaders absorb stress; when staff miss an unclear standard, the speaker places the burden on the leader's communication first. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Interview discipline (anticipated questions, murder boards, one thesis, truth sandwich) also serves high-stakes internal briefings. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
