## Overview
OPM senior advisor Noah Peters interviews Vince Haley, Assistant to the President for Domestic Policy, on how policy moves from campaign to executive order to agency execution. Haley describes the White House coordination process and offers management frameworks he attributes to Newt Gingrich.

## Key points
- Haley says policy begins with campaign promises; an executive order sets priorities, is bound to existing law, and does not make law. [01:01]
- Contested ideas move from a policy coordinating committee to deputies, then cabinet principals, then the President. [04:08]
- He argues agencies should carry out the president's lawful priorities; ideas that advance them are welcome, contrary priorities are not. [06:13]
- He cites the Geneva Consensus Declaration as an idea that began with an agency employee. [08:18]
- He says SES members should track presidential priorities continually and repeat them to their teams. [14:30]
- Frameworks: listen, learn, help, lead; vision, metrics, strategies, projects; choose consequential projects on short timelines. [18:37]
- Effective agencies ask what success looks like, over-communicate, write clearly, name a few priorities, and measure achievements rather than activity. [24:48]

## Executive takeaways
- Define the achievement, not the activity, when setting goals and reporting progress. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- A tiered escalation ladder, settling at working level before going up, is a workable model for decision rights among peer leaders. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Executive orders operate within law, so execution plans should trace back to legal authority. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
