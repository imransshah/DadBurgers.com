## Overview
Austin Raynor, a senior advisor at OPM, explains orders on discretionary spending, data sharing for fraud detection, energy regulation sunsets, grant oversight and public-facing services. His common thread is a zero-based approach: each expense and each regulation needs a current justification.

## Key points
- EO 14222 requires a system recording a one-line justification for each covered payment, a review of contracts and grants, new contract-entry guidance, and a review of leases [01:08]
- HHS runs a Defend the Spend pilot on its payment system for recording justifications [04:11]
- EO 14243 asks agencies to remove unjustified barriers to sharing data for waste, fraud and abuse detection and to review classification policies for overbreadth [05:12]
- Raynor recommends adding a waste, fraud and abuse routine use to system of records notices, covering sharing within and between agencies [08:15]
- The Radical Transparency memorandum has agencies post details of canceled grants and discontinued obligations, under written guidance on what is published [14:21]
- The Impoundment Control Act provides a process when an appropriation has no good use [16:23]
- EO 14270 adds one-year conditional sunsets to covered energy regulations at named agencies; extensions run up to five years each, and permitting is excluded [17:24]
- EO 14332 adds political review of funding notices and awards, termination for convenience in grants, and justification before drawdowns [24:33]
- EO 14338 creates a National Design Studio and Chief Design Officer, with first results due July 4, 2026 [30:40]

## Executive takeaways
- Require a current justification for recurring spend and inherited rules instead of renewing by default (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- Keep privacy notices and data-sharing authorities current so lawful sharing is ready when needed (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- Judge digital services through the user's eyes: easy to use and pleasant to look at (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
