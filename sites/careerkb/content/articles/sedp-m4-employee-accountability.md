## Overview
Colleen Heller Stein, Executive Director of the CHCO Council at OPM, explains the two Title 5 accountability tools, how to choose between them, and how employees can contest actions. The speaker stresses that HR does much of the heavy lifting, while supervisors own monitoring, documentation and addressing problems.

## Key points
- The rules apply to employees, not contractors; probationary employees can generally be terminated without most of these steps. [04:14]
- The proposing official is usually the first-line supervisor; the deciding official is usually the second-level manager. [05:15]
- Chapter 43 actions tie to a standard in the performance plan and require a 30-business-day PIP before a proposed demotion or removal. [08:22]
- When an employee cannot or will not improve, the speaker says accountability is necessary: "Ignoring the issue will never make it better" (Heller Stein). [11:26]
- Chapter 75 actions cover poor performance or misconduct affecting the efficiency of the service, with no PIP: proposal, response, decision. [11:26]
- Misconduct goes through chapter 75; poor performance can use either. Chapter 43 is slower but earns managers significant deference on appeal; chapter 75 is faster but needs a preponderance of the evidence. [14:31]
- Collective bargaining agreements add steps [15:31], and arbitration can reverse a removal. In one case the employee won after the agency had restructured, and finding a new position took over a year. [16:33]
- Monitoring relies on clear objective standards, regular check-ins, KPI and work-product reviews, and 360 feedback [21:43]. Pitfalls are inconsistent standards, thin documentation and micromanagement. [22:46]
- Employee channels: administrative and negotiated grievances, EEO complaints, the Office of Special Counsel, the Department of Labor (military service) and the MSPB. [25:50]

## Executive takeaways
- Leaders need to know which chapter fits and which forums can review an action. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- The speaker counsels holding employees with similar functions to similar standards; inconsistency is the first of three pitfalls she names. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
- Early HR and counsel involvement, plus prompt action, keeps problems from escalating. (ECQ4 Leading People)
