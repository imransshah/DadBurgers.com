## Overview
Jason Parman, Principal Deputy Associate Director of HR Solutions at OPM, speaks with a host in two parts: organization design (Part 1), then workforce reshaping, reduction in force (RIF), and change management (Part 2). Timestamps restart in each part.

## Key points
- Part 1: Organization design aligns structure, processes, people, and funding to strategy; it is more than an org chart. [03:02]
- Part 1: No structure is best in all cases. Each solves some problems and creates others, so form should follow function. [06:05]
- Part 1: Outdated or unclear mission, vision, and values can lead to strategy drift and trust deficits. [08:06]
- Part 1: Misalignment shows up as unclear roles, distant decision makers, bottlenecks, and friction with enabling functions, including IT. [19:15]
- Part 2: Reshaping follows changes in function or funding [02:01]. The speaker calls a RIF a blunt last resort and lists VERA, VSIP, directed reassignments, and deferred resignation as alternatives. [04:04]
- Part 2: A RIF moves from data validation [08:09] and a defined future state [09:10] through mitigation [10:10], competitive areas and levels, and retention registers built with OPM's Workforce Reshaping Tool [11:11]. Appeals should be expected, so the process must be clearly documented and defensible throughout. [12:12]
- Part 2: Communication must reach first- and second-line supervisors, not only all-hands channels; the speaker echoes the host's phrase "clarity is kindness." [17:17]
- Part 2: Outplacement [19:17], clear benefits guidance [20:18], EAP [21:20], and attention to survivor guilt help departing and remaining staff. [24:23]
- Part 2: Change management is a structured process for changing behaviors; the speaker names the Kotter and ADKAR models. [28:26]

## Executive takeaways
- Use reshaping tools before separations, and align structure to current function and funding. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- RIF outcomes depend on accurate workforce data and a documented process that complies with law and regulation. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- Major change lands through first- and second-line supervisors and a chosen change model, not through announcements alone. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- The speaker says small points of friction with enabling functions, IT among them, add up to lost time and lower morale, and high error rates after a new platform signal outdated skills. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
