## Overview
OPM senior advisor Noah Peters explains recent workforce policy changes and what senior executives must do to implement them. The stated theme is accountability: faster removal where law allows, ratings that differentiate, and hiring based on tested skills.

## Key points
- Schedule Policy/Career covers roles 5 U.S.C. 7511 describes as confidential or policy-influencing, mostly GS-13 to GS-15. Employees stay in Title 5 and are hired on merit, but can be removed by letter without MSPB appeal. [02:01]
- OPM says it will monitor for retaliation and prohibited discrimination. [14:12]
- Probation now requires a pre-end performance conversation and affirmative agency certification; dismissal can rest on agency needs. [18:16]
- Rating changes: a supervisory accountability element, eliminating level 2, and ending formal union grievances over ratings. [26:29]
- OPM may set standardized distributions; the speaker anticipates an agency-wide 30% cap on the top two levels. [30:32]
- Every competitive hiring action needs at least one assessment; the rule of many lets agencies rank and select from more candidates. [35:37]
- Strategic hiring committees approve vacancies and selections and favor shared certificates. [43:46]
- In-office work is the default; telework accommodations can be revisited and need supporting documentation. [47:51]

## Executive takeaways
- Use tested skills, not self-described qualifications, to select. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
- Re-baselining ratings is a change-management task: level 3 means fully successful. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Reduced-process authorities still require documentation and compliance with anti-retaliation rules. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
- Technical roles, including IT operations, can use structured interviews and technical assessments. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
