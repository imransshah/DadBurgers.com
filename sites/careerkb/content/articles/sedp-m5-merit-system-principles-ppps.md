## Overview
Kim Steed, a Deputy Associate Director in OPM's Merit System Accountability and Compliance office, walks through the merit system principles (MSPs) and prohibited personnel practices (PPPs). She describes the MSPs as an ethos for management and the PPPs as guardrails.

## Key points
- The MSPs trace from the Garfield assassination and the Pendleton Act to the Civil Service Reform Act, which set out nine principles. [01:09]
- Fair treatment means judging applicants on knowledge, skills, and abilities [02:09]. At one agency no veterans made the certificates although many met the ranking criteria; the speaker attributes this to an atmosphere that saw veterans as less competent, and OPM worked with the agency on veterans' preference rights. [03:09]
- Performance management should set expectations in conversation and in writing; OPM reviews look for similar ratings for similar work across gender, location, and grade. [05:12]
- Training selection should follow clear, published criteria. [06:13]
- OPM evaluates agency HR operations and refers violations to the Office of Special Counsel [09:18]. In one case, crediting only attorney experience for non-attorney jobs led to an investigation, required training, and fines. [10:18]
- There are now 14 PPPs; nepotism appears often in evaluations. [12:26]
- On whistleblower reprisal risk, the speaker advises documenting everything and working with HR. [14:28]
- Discouraging employees from applying elsewhere obstructs competition [16:31]. Recognition that favors headquarters over field staff can amount to favoritism; remote details can widen access. [17:33]
- Consequences range from a letter in the file to removal, plus organization-wide training for systemic problems. [20:38]

## Executive takeaways
- The speaker's core advice is to be objective and fair in every workforce decision, use clear and communicated criteria for selections such as training, and document actions with HR. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
- The speaker describes the MSPs as an ethos for management and the PPPs, defined in the U.S. Code, as guardrails; she adds that federal employees are apolitical and serve whatever administration is in power. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- The speaker says headquarters staff often get larger and more frequent recognition because they work on high-profile projects, and suggests details, including remote details, so field staff get the same exposure. (ECQ3 Merit & Competence)
