## Overview
Jolene, Assistant Attorney General for Administration at the U.S. Department of Justice, discusses leading change from a mission-support role. Her main case is the department's unified financial management system (UFMS), which she took over after three failed attempts.

## Key points
- She separates a public-service job from a public servant's mission commitment and screens for the latter when hiring. [01:07]
- Mission-support staff understand their impact better after meeting operating components; a project retreat at Quantico built buy-in with the FBI. [04:13]
- Leading through ambiguity takes authentic communication at every level, one-on-one talks with the most resistant employees, and respectful exit paths for those who will not support the change. [08:18]
- People can be inspired but not forced; the UFMS team used shared themes and humor to build a common vision. [11:22]
- Trust with leadership comes from delivering repeatedly and reporting problems early with alternatives; a surprise is worse than a delay. [22:44]
- The business case rested on controls and reliable information, not savings. It was re-sold after each leadership change, ownership was consolidated, and the new system was made non-optional; she calls this being "a strategic bully with a smile." [27:51]
- Accountability depends on clear deliverables and a realistic milestone chart. [30:57]
- Budget staff belong in strategy from the start; an early deployment lacked infrastructure because the CIO's office was not involved. [33:03]
- Strategic plans work best as three or four goals on one page, with two-year priority goals and the budget aligned to them. [37:12]

## Executive takeaways
- Fund modernization through an early CFO–CIO partnership and a multi-year financing plan that can outlast administration changes. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- Sustain a transformation by restating its business case to each new leadership team and holding all parties to a visible timeline. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Lead change by owning it, engaging resisters individually, and placing poor-fit staff where they can do well. (ECQ4 Leading People)
