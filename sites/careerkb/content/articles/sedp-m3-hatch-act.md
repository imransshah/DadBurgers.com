## Overview
A U.S. Office of Special Counsel senior counsel explains the Hatch Act's purpose, prohibitions and enforcement, and how it applies to senior executives. Career SES members are further restricted in every agency. Political neutrality, the speaker stresses, is not policy neutrality.

## Key points
- Aims: nonpartisan administration, protection from political coercion at work, and merit-based advancement. [01:09]
- Prohibited: political activity on duty or in federal space, soliciting or receiving contributions, using authority to affect elections, partisan candidacy, and pressuring people with business before the agency. [02:10]
- OSC advises, investigates and prosecutes; MSPB decides penalties, up to removal and debarment; OPM issues regulations. [03:14]
- Career SES members may not share campaign posts or help with fundraisers, but may post their own views off duty. [10:24]
- On duty includes telework in paid status; issue discussion is allowed. [13:29]
- The fundraising ban applies at all times, with no family-and-friends exception. [18:37]
- Supervisor requests to subordinates about votes or money are inherently coercive; cited cases ended in removal. [22:45]
- Rank is weighed in penalties, so senior leaders face higher stakes. [32:58]

## Executive takeaways
- Treat any political request to a subordinate as off-limits. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- SES entry brings the strictest tier, so off-duty activity needs a fresh check. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
- Many cited violations ran through email, social media and chat on duty; acceptable-use guidance and election-season reminders reduce exposure. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
- Leaders' tolerated rhetoric becomes office culture. (ECQ4 Leading People)
