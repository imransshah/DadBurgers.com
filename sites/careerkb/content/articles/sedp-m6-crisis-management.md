## Overview
Christian Earhart (spelling per captions ⚑ verify), Deputy Assistant Secretary in the State Department's Bureau of Population, Refugees and Migration and a Diplomatic Security Service special agent by background, discusses crisis management with host Rebecca. The session links decision theory to field examples, then covers composure and team resilience.

## Key points
- Crisis management begins before the crisis. Earhart applies the Washington Post test (would this response hold up in public?) and keeps a network of advisers. [02:14]
- Executives manage down with clear tasking and up with context, history and options; career SES members provide continuity across administrations. [04:16]
- Triage by life safety, financial, national security and public impact; decide immediately what must be decided, then evolve the response. [06:18], [08:20]
- When overruled by a senior official, the job shifts to carrying out that decision safely, using relationships built in advance. [09:21]–[11:26]
- The recognition-primed decision model means arriving ready with planned first responses; the OODA loop is a repeating cycle of new information and adjusted decisions. [14:31], [15:31]
- The host ties this to bounded rationality and satisficing: decide on the best information available. [19:36]
- As a crisis widens to other agencies, decisions slow down and need a clear chain of command. [22:40]
- Stress tools: depersonalize the issue, consult mentors, step out during long crises, and project calm. Leaders who center themselves or exclude the team erode trust. [24:44]–[27:48]
- Resilience comes from knowing each person's baseline, natural team rapport, using people's strengths, and a deep bench for rotation. [29:50]–[35:57]

## Executive takeaways
- Make the first decision on partial information and adjust as the situation develops. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Build trust, rapport and bench strength before a crisis, and model composure during one. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Test decisions against public scrutiny, and know general counsel and interagency contacts before a crisis so red tape does not block the right decision. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
