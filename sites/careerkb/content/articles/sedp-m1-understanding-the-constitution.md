## Overview
Dr. Shaun Rieley interviews Dr. Ronald Pestritto, graduate dean at Hillsdale College, on how the Constitution allocates power and how its working has changed since the founding. The session closes with Pestritto's view of where senior executives sit in that structure.

## Key points
- The Constitution sets up a government of enumerated powers: a power not named is presumed not granted, unlike plenary monarchic systems. [01:10]
- The Preamble, the 10th Amendment and Federalist 84 reflect that logic; the 9th Amendment keeps the Bill of Rights from being read as a complete list. [04:16]
- The framers rejected legislative supremacy after 1780s state legislatures overreached; separation of powers guards against majority faction. [09:20]
- Checks such as the veto and Senate confirmation make separation real; without them, Madison warned, it is only a parchment barrier. [12:25]
- Federalism per Federalist 39 is partly federal and partly national, each level supreme in its own sphere; Article IV, Section 4 lists three federal duties to states. [14:28]
- Article II vests the executive power, a more general power, in the President, while Article I lists specific legislative powers. [18:33]
- Pestritto argues Article III limits courts to cases or controversies, with no general power of constitutional review. [20:35]
- Pestritto argues senior executives exercise part of the President's power, that agencies are statutory, and that no independent fourth branch has a constitutional basis. [36:51]

## Executive takeaways
- The course asks executives to establish early where authority comes from, who else has a role and what limits apply; Pestritto places agency existence in statute and executive power in the President. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- The course's closing step: before a high-risk initiative advances, have counsel or policy staff map its touchpoints with Congress, the courts, states and individual rights. (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- The course frames the goal as delivering mission results while preserving the legitimacy of the process, and treats the Constitution as a guide before decisions, not only a legal backstop afterward. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
