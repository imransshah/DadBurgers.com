## Overview
Troy Edgar, Deputy Secretary of Homeland Security and former DHS Chief Financial Officer, talks with host Rebecca about the political-career relationship, federal budgeting, financial systems modernization and innovation. He draws on corporate finance experience and his DHS roles.

## Key points
- Political-career trust starts at zero. Career executives should research an incoming appointee beforehand, offer options with pros and cons, and fully support the principal's choice; the speaker links pressure such as Schedule F to a perception that career staff were not on board. [02:01], [04:02], [08:06]
- Executives manage three budget years at once: current-year execution under OMB apportionment, next year's budget with Congress, and a strategic third year. Formulation takes 18 to 24 months. [14:12]
- Executives outside finance should read their public budget justifications and know how to resource their mission; Edgar calls the budget director the most important job in government. [16:14], [19:15]
- The budget is how policy gets executed. Plan five years out and fund large projects in phases with interim wins; a DHS modernization running since 2012 and on GAO's High Risk List left staff demoralized. [20:16], [22:18]–[24:21]
- Culture levers include aligning each leader's two- and six-year goals with the executive's, and informal recurring community-of-interest meetings. [26:23], [28:25]
- A common accounting structure let one financial process serve all DHS components. Modernizations stall when requirements are set years before work starts and contractors vet requirements the government no longer manages. [40:38], [42:40]
- OMB's QSMO effort, as described, sets a standard finance-system model with pre-negotiated vendors to normalize bids and control cost. [45:43]
- VA automated claims evaluation, validated it against a union-agreed manual sample, and routed cases below 90% confidence to manual review, cutting processing from about seven months to three weeks. [48:45]–[51:49]
- Plan backward: define success five years out, identify the critical path, and share priorities on one page. [57:57]–[59:58]

## Executive takeaways
- Phase multiyear investments into fundable increments with visible results, and stop projects that are not delivering. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- Plan backward from a defined end state and make the critical path visible to staff. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Involve unions when automation changes staff work, as VA's executives did. (ECQ4 Leading People)
