## Overview
Stephen Billy, senior advisor at the Office of Management and Budget, discusses workforce accountability from the organization-wide view. He covers culture change, cascading priorities, outcome measurement, and the difference between poor performance and misconduct.

## Key points
- Billy cites federal employee and MSPB surveys showing discontent with how poor performers are handled, and guidance that top bonuses go to top achievers rather than being spread evenly. [03:01]
- Career SES and political officials work together; in the grants executive order example, political officials review while career SES run programs day to day. [06:04]
- Billy has seen daily ten-minute stand-ups work for spotting efficiencies, such as reports that no longer serve a purpose, with a team then assigned to address them. [08:05]
- Tools discussed include the PMA, the merit hiring plan and OPM performance guidance [10:07]; aligned CBJs and the President's Management Council [12:10]–[13:10]; and the deferred resignation program. [15:13]
- Culture change relies on open dialogue, explaining the why, public recognition, and leading by example. [17:16]
- Billy says agencies have been given guidance that strategic plans will look different; he describes boiling them down to about ten pages in plain language [22:19], cascading from executive orders and the PMA to program teams. [23:19]
- Outputs are not outcomes: grants signed is an output, the change achieved is the outcome. [26:22]
- Metrics differ by program (wait times, self-service use, per-outcome cost, surveys) [29:26]; data collected for its own sake can be cut. [31:27]
- Poor performance is missing stated job expectations; misconduct is broader, such as attendance or treatment of coworkers. [40:35]

## Executive takeaways
- Determine each program's outcomes and metrics first, then decide what data to collect; the speaker calls using outcomes to track accountability the most critical step. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Differentiate awards and act on poor performance; the speaker says high performers leave when others' slack falls on them. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- The speaker advises tying change proposals to the strategic plan, the secretary's priorities, an executive order requirement, and the budget outlook. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
