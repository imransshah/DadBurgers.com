## Overview
OPM senior advisor Noah Peters interviews Vince Haley, Assistant to the President for Domestic Policy, on how policy moves from campaign to executive order to agency execution. Haley describes the White House coordination process and offers management frameworks he attributes to Newt Gingrich.

## Key points
- Haley says policy begins with campaign promises [01:01]; an executive order sets priorities, is bound to existing law, and does not make law. [02:03]
- Contested ideas move from a policy coordinating committee [04:08] to deputies, then cabinet principals, then the President. [06:13]
- He argues agencies should carry out the president's lawful priorities; ideas that advance them are welcome, contrary priorities are not. [07:17]
- He cites the Geneva Consensus Declaration as an idea that began with an agency employee. [08:18]
- He says SES members should track presidential priorities continually [14:30] and repeat them to their teams. [17:37]
- Frameworks he attributes to Gingrich: listen, learn, help, lead [19:39]; vision, metrics, strategies, projects [20:45]; choose big, consequential projects on short timelines. [21:46]
- Effective agencies, he says, ask what success looks like and over-communicate [24:48], and write clearly [26:54]; leaders should name a few priorities [27:54] and measure achievements rather than activity. [28:54]

## Executive takeaways
- Define the achievement, not the activity, when setting goals and reporting progress. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Haley says leaders must name priorities and repeat them to their people, and that listening to others surfaces the information needed to execute. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Haley describes an executive order as a statement of priority that is bound by law and does not make law. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
