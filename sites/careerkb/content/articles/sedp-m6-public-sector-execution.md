## Overview
Jolene, Assistant Attorney General for Administration at the U.S. Department of Justice, discusses leading change from a mission-support role. Her main case is the department's unified financial management system (UFMS), which she took over after three failed attempts.

## Key points
- She separates a public-service job from a public servant's mission commitment and screens for the latter when hiring. [02:11]
- Mission-support staff understand their impact better after meeting operating components [04:13]; a Quantico retreat, held when FBI adoption stalled, gave the implementation team an appreciation of the FBI's work. [07:15]
- Leading through ambiguity takes authentic communication at every level [09:20], one-on-one talks with the most resistant employees [10:21], and respectful exit paths for those who will not support the change. [11:22]
- People can be inspired but not forced; the UFMS team used shared themes and humor to build a common vision. [12:25]
- Trust with leadership comes from delivering repeatedly and reporting problems early [22:44]; she expects alternatives and treats a surprise as worse than a delay. [24:49]
- Split ownership that let components avoid adoption was ended [27:51]. The business case was never only about money but about controls and reliable information; it was re-sold after each leadership change [28:53], and the new system was made non-optional; she calls this being "a strategic bully with a smile." [29:55]
- Accountability depends on clear deliverables and a realistic milestone chart. [31:59]
- Budget staff belong in strategy from the start [33:03]; an early deployment lacked infrastructure because the CIO's office was not involved. [35:11]
- She advises strategic plans of three or four goals that fit on one page, with two-year priority goals and the budget aligned to them. [38:16]

## Executive takeaways
- Bring budget staff into modernization strategy early, treat the CFO and CIO as partners, and design a financial strategy that keeps a multi-year effort funded across administrations. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- Sustain a transformation by restating its business case to each new leadership team and holding all parties to a clear, realistic timeline. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Lead change by owning it, engaging resisters individually, and placing poor-fit staff where they can do well. (ECQ4 Leading People)
