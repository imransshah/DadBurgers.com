## Overview
Stephen Billy, senior advisor at the Office of Management and Budget, discusses workforce accountability from the organization-wide view. He covers culture change, cascading priorities, outcome measurement, and the difference between poor performance and misconduct.

## Key points
- Billy cites employee and MSPB surveys showing discontent with how poor performers are handled, and says top awards should go to top performers rather than being spread evenly. [02:01]
- Career SES and political officials work together; in the grants executive order example, political officials review while career SES run programs day to day. [06:04]
- Daily ten-minute stand-ups can surface reports and tasks that serve no purpose. [08:05]
- Levers named include the PMA, the merit hiring plan, OPM performance guidance, aligned CBJs, the President's Management Council, and the deferred resignation program. [10:07]
- Culture change relies on open dialogue, explaining the why, public recognition, and leading by example. [17:16]
- Per Billy, strategic plans should run about ten plain-language pages and cascade from executive orders and the PMA to program teams. [22:19]
- Outputs are not outcomes: grants signed is an output, the change achieved is the outcome. [26:22]
- Metrics differ by program (wait times, self-service use, per-outcome cost, surveys); data collected for its own sake can be cut. [31:27]
- Poor performance is missing stated job expectations; misconduct is broader, such as attendance or treatment of coworkers. [40:35]

## Executive takeaways
- Define each program's outcome and metric before adding data collection or tools. (ECQ5 Achieving Results)
- Differentiate awards and act on poor performance; the speaker says high performers leave when others' slack falls on them. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Tie change proposals to the strategic plan, an executive order requirement, and the budget. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
