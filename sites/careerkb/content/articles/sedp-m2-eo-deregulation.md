## Overview
Jeff Clark, acting administrator of OIRA at OMB, explains centralized review of agency rules and four executive orders that push deregulation. He stresses defensible records and early consultation with OIRA and the Justice Department.

## Key points
- OIRA grew out of the Paperwork Reduction Act and reviews rules under EO 12866, successor to EO 12291 [02:01]
- OIRA first decides whether a rule is significant ($100 million or more in impact, or major policy or legal questions), then reviews authority, record and logic, gathers other agencies' comments, and iterates via pass backs [08:09]
- EO 12866 allows 90 days plus a 30-day extension; Clark says OIRA aims for a month or less [13:14]
- Courts review rules under APA section 706; arbitrary-and-capricious findings can lead to vacatur or remand [15:17]
- Direct final and interim final rules let new policy take effect sooner [16:18]
- EO 14192 requires ten deregulatory actions per new regulatory action and net savings by dollar magnitude [18:20]
- EO 14219 and an April 9, 2025 memorandum direct review against ten Supreme Court decisions, including Michigan v. EPA (costs) and Loper Bright (best reading of the statute, ending Chevron deference) [22:26]
- EO 14267 targets regulations that create barriers to entry [26:30]
- EO 14294 favors voluntary and civil compliance and reserves criminal penalties for intentional, serious violations [29:34]

## Executive takeaways
- A rule or policy is only as strong as its record and statutory basis; involve counsel early (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
- Count deregulatory impact by magnitude, not just number of actions (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- Clark invites executives to consult OIRA desk officers and DOJ litigators before a rule is final (ECQ1 Rule of Law & Founding Principles)
