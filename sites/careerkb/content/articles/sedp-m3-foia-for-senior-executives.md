## Overview
An OPM senior advisor interviews the acting solicitor of the Department of the Interior (name captioned as Greg Cerzan, ⚑ verify) on what FOIA requires of senior executives. The session covers scope, exemptions, deadlines, careful communications, and modernization.

## Key points
- FOIA is framed as an accountability tool that builds public trust. [01:08]
- Assume everything recorded is accessible, including emails and texts. [02:10]
- Exemptions cover national security, privacy, trade secrets, and deliberative material on work in progress, much of which may be released after a policy is implemented. [03:10]
- The FOIA office tasks employees with searches and applies exemptions and redactions; employees produce records. [04:12]
- The speaker says agencies have 20 days to respond and can owe attorneys' fees when they miss it. [06:14]
- Request volume is the main barrier to compliance. [08:18]
- There is no exemption for embarrassing content; the speaker advises never writing anything one would not want on a newspaper's front page. [10:23]
- After an unflattering release, coach without creating fear. [14:29]
- The speaker hopes AI will search and redact records, and urges keeping records electronic. [16:31]

## Executive takeaways
- FOIA compliance is a legal duty regardless of how a release will look. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
- Leaders set communication norms: clear and careful, not fearful. (ECQ4 Leading People)
- Search capability across email, chat and mobile text is what makes deadlines achievable, and automation is the main lever against volume. (ECQ2 Driving Efficiency)
- Where AI redaction is proposed, governance should decide where human review remains. (ECQ1 Rule of Law)
